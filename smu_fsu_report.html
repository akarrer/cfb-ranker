Technical reflection

  • Absolute accuracy was excellent. A 1.5-point margin error against a season MAE of ~14 points is the model operating at its ceiling. The recalibrated scale produced a genuinely tight line for a genuinely tight game — important evidence the 1.6× widening didn't over-spread close matchups, only mismatches.
  • The "edge" was an illusion. The model claimed 1.5 points of value on Florida State +3. The game landed exactly on 3 — a push. A 1.5-point edge sits well inside both the model's own error bar and the market's precision; it was never a real signal.
  • The market was perfect and the model was merely close. Market error 0.0 vs model 1.5. This is the same lesson as the weekend's 43% ATS: matching Vegas' scale is not beating Vegas. Sub-3-point disagreements should be treated as noise, not plays.
  • The results engine behaved correctly by barely moving. A 3-point road win by a slight favorite is almost exactly "as expected," so a ±0.2 nudge and zero rank movement is the right response — not a failure to react. Contrast the Michigan case: the engine only moves teams when reality genuinely diverges from expectation.
Practical takeaway: raise the actionable threshold. Given a ~14-point margin MAE, only disagreements north of roughly a touchdown carry information — and even those have been coin flips so far. The model's value right now is ranking and winner-picking (90% SU on the weekend), not spread betting.
